# Prior Events & Inherited Context

What happened before today that shapes what we build.

## 2026-07-21 — Anchorage community session

From the event description:

> A full room in Anchorage spent two hours on one question: **What is AI doing to
> me, my kids, and my community?** They finished the night by naming where Alaska
> should start, and the answer was **education and access**.

That session's output *is* today's brief. Today is the build phase of a
conversation that already happened without us.

## Distilled — problems named

Ranked by how often the room returned to them.

1. **Kids, and adults who don't know what to tell them.** The single most concrete
   card: a parent of a 9-, 12-, and 15-year-old asking *what do I say to my kids
   about how to prepare?* Echoed by a grandparent worried about unsupervised use,
   and by "will youth be able to differentiate between facts and AI?"
2. **Schools that are already out of capacity.** "Overwhelmed + underfunded." The
   ask is not "AI in the classroom" — it's *how do you train staff to teach
   critical thinking without adding load.* Any solution that costs a teacher time
   is dead on arrival.
3. **No district-level policy or best practices.** The Education vote card is
   almost entirely governance: school district policy, best practices for the
   district, a wholistic approach to the education workforce, ethical use.
4. **Access is physical, not conceptual.** The Access card broke down to
   *hardware, software, workshops/classes* — devices and in-person teaching, not
   awareness campaigns.
5. **Data and knowledge sovereignty, especially Alaska Native.** Recurring and
   specific: 570+ tribal nations, Indigenous art appropriation, extraction framing,
   "missing community input — including Alaska Native communities." Nobody in the
   room treated this as a footnote.
6. **Environmental cost, framed locally.** Not generic — water draw, electricity
   rates, land use, fire hazards, *subsistence activities*, disproportionate rural
   impact.
7. **The career ladder.** Several cards on juniors never becoming seniors, and
   entry-level hiring drying up.
8. **Cognitive atrophy.** Attention span, "the less a person has to learn, the
   more they give up," LLM addiction akin to social media.

## Distilled — ideas proposed

The room mostly asked questions rather than proposing builds. The few actual
proposals:

- A **volunteer AI community** tackling "greater good" projects — the card names
  Anchorage homelessness and says "I would tackle this FIRST."
- **Events that stimulate curiosity** and build community understanding — i.e.
  more of what today is.
- **Training resources for teachers and districts**, explicitly because budgets
  are strained.
- **AI for accessibility** — "hopeful about how AI can help people with disabilities."
- One craft-level ask: how to **author e-learning content with AI without losing
  your design values, principles, and voice** (from an instructional designer).

## Distilled — constraints / cautions raised

Treat these as design constraints on whatever we build today:

- **Don't add work to teachers or school staff.** Stated as the central tension.
- **Don't speak for Alaska Native communities.** "Missing community input" was a
  vote card. If our project touches Indigenous knowledge or data, the honest move
  is to build the thing that *invites* input rather than one that presumes it.
- **Critical thinking is the goal, not answers.** Multiple cards fear AI that
  makes people comfortable rather than stronger. A tool that hands over an answer
  fails the room's own test.
- **Trust requires visible safeguards.** "Using a tool without knowing or trusting
  the safeguards that exist or do not exist."
- **Environmental and cost honesty.** Someone asked what a single query costs.
- **Equity of access is the point.** "How do we democratize the benefit of AI?"

## What this implies for today

The brief says build for *learning, teaching, and thinking clearly*. Cross-referenced
with the cards, the highest-signal, least-crowded openings are:

- Something a **parent** can actually use to have the conversation with a 9/12/15-
  year-old. Highest-frequency, most concrete, most demo-able to a 6:00 PM room that
  contains parents.
- Something that gives a **district** a starting policy / best-practices draft
  without a committee — because "no policy" was the Education card's whole content.
- Something that makes **access** concrete: where to physically get hands on a
  machine and a class in Anchorage.

See `../01-project/charter.md` for how these got weighed.
